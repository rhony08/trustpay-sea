Brief about the idea

One place to check who you're paying — before you pay.

TrustPay SEA is a community-driven payment-risk and reputation registry for Southeast Asia: search any bank account, phone number, or QR code and instantly see the registered holder, an explainable risk score, and the community's report history.

Caller-ID for payments

Enter an account or scan a QR (QRIS, DuitNow, PromptPay, PayNow, VietQR, QR Ph) and see the holder name + risk before transferring.

Reviews for scams

Victims file redacted reports; the community upvotes and flags; legitimate owners can claim, dispute, and freeze their score.

Trust that's explainable

An open 0–100 score with factor breakdown and exact-match name disclosure — useful, without becoming a doxxing list.

The problem

Instant payments are irreversible. Scammers exploit the gap.

⚡

No chargeback

QRIS, DuitNow, PromptPay, PayNow and bank transfers settle instantly — once sent, funds are gone. No card-style dispute to fall back on.

🎭

Money-mule accounts

Scammers say "pay Admin Siti" — but the account belongs to a rented mule, "Bambang". Victims discover the mismatch too late.

🔍

No way to check

Before paying, there's no consumer tool to verify an account or QR. Government blacklists exist but coverage is partial.

Reported scam losses across Southeast Asia run into the billions of dollars annually — concentrated in e-commerce, fake jobs, and investment fraud.

The insight

Payment safety is a two-sided reputation problem.

Victims need to warn others

Like Google Reviews — but for the exact destination account, not a business listing. Every report hardens the registry for everyone.

Payers need a caller-ID

Like Truecaller — enter the account/QR and instantly see the registered holder name, risk score, and history before transferring.

The core mechanic: Recipient Name Matcher

You enter the name the seller told you ("Siti"). We check it against the bank-registered holder ("Bambang S.") and flash a mule alert on mismatch. One check stops the most common scam before money moves.

How it works

From a single lookup to a hardened registry.

1

Lookup. The browser decodes the QR for instant preview; the server re-decodes, validates the EMVCo CRC, and canonicalizes the entity.

2

Score. An explainable weighted risk model returns a 0–100 score, band, and factor breakdown — no black box.

3

Report. A submission enters an async pipeline: fail-closed PII redaction → OCR + tamper + duplicate checks → typology + NER.

4

Adjudicate. Analysts review under four-eyes dual control; every trust-altering action is written to a hash-chained audit log.

AI

Multimodal, grounded, and fail-closed.

Slip forensics

A vision model extracts recipient/amount/ref, detects PII regions, and flags tampering — in one call. Redaction is drawn locally and is fail-closed: a failure never publishes unredacted evidence.

Grounded reasoning

Report/OCR text is treated as untrusted data, never instructions. Output is JSON-schema validated against one canonical taxonomy, and entity summaries are grounded to traceable facts.

Scam-message analyzer

Paste a chat and get social-engineering signals plus extracted payment coordinates — analyzing the artifact, not a global fact-check database (which has poor local coverage).

Provider-agnostic

AI_PROVIDER=mock|gcp|openrouter. Runs with zero keys via local heuristics and lights up live Gemini when configured — never blocking a demo.

Trust & compliance

Built to survive legal and privacy scrutiny.

No cleartext account storage

Accounts are indexed only as KMS-keyed HMAC; public surfaces are masked by default.

Asymmetric PII redaction

Victim identity is protected; the scammer's destination stays clear so payers can verify it.

Exact-match disclosure only

Holder names are revealed only on a full exact lookup — never in bulk listings (ADR-016).

Human-in-the-loop

Four-eyes dual control + WebAuthn MFA + hash-chained audit for destructive actions.

Region-pinned to Southeast Asia · designed for UU PDP / PDPA compliance · statutory non-custodial disclaimers in the UI.
